# Zen Compact Transparent HDR Guard v0.2.2 — HDR glass style with native Zia compositor

Adds a separately configurable, **opt-in** HDR glass appearance while preserving the previously tested v0.2.1 SDR behavior.

## Feature
- New Sine checkbox: **HDR 影片使用 HDR Guard 自訂毛玻璃**, **default OFF**.
- With Zia 2.118 `Blur over video` **ON**, confirmed HDR streams can use HDR Guard's custom sidebar color, transparency, blur strength, and size **only while the sidebar/toolbar is displayed**.
- **No unconditional `will-change: transform` or other video-compositor promotion.** This MOD only styles the exposed sidebar; Zia controls the browser video layer. No video brightness filters.
- With the new HDR checkbox OFF, HDR reverts immediately to the pre-existing Zia behavior. SDR appearance and optional SDR checkbox remain independent.
- Zia Blur over video OFF, unknown-stream classification, missing visible video, or hidden sidebar will not apply the custom HDR surface.
- Retains YouTube/Bilibili stream recognition, generic HTML5, split panes, all existing manual SDR fallback rules, Site Folders compatibility, and original Transparent Mode appearance.

## How to test on macOS 27.2 / OLED
1. Keep Zia v2.118's **See-through surfaces keep blurring a video behind them** enabled.
2. In Sine update HDR Guard to **0.2.2**, restart Zen, and enable the new HDR checkbox.
3. Play a confirmed HDR stream. Sidebar hidden: video should retain its original HDR look. Sidebar shown: custom HDR Guard frosted glass should replace Zia's native sidebar styling; darker HDR video is acceptable. Sidebar hidden again: video must recover.
4. Repeat with an SDR video; it must retain the v0.2.1 appearance. Check Zia/native tab cards and Site Folders v0.7.6 preview.
5. Toggle the new HDR checkbox OFF and verify a complete immediate visual rollback to Zia for HDR.

Zia blur-over-video may cause HDR video to dim/lose HDR while overlays are shown because of Gecko/macOS compositing; this cannot be prevented by CSS alone. **GitHub Node tests validate state and CSS scoping, not final hardware HDR brightness recovery.**
